# SOL-032 · Decimals assumed, not read

Amount math hardcodes a scale (`1_000_000`, `10u64.pow(9)`) instead of reading `mint.decimals` → a 6-vs-9 mint misprices 1000×. Fix: read `mint.decimals`; normalize first.

**Enforcement — review-only.** A judgment-call class with no deterministic pattern: it lives in the standard's review checklist and in your AI agent's [rules file](../../integrations), so the model reviews for it.

**Value tier — HIGH.** HIGH — high-value; surfaced even at a strict submission floor

**Baseline severity — high.** A starting point, not a verdict: calibrate by preconditions × access level (take the lower) per [SEVERITY.md](../../SEVERITY.md).

**Reachability anchor.** To confirm this is exploitable (not merely present), cite the amount/price computation applying a hardcoded decimals scale where a second mint (or a different-decimals mint) can enter the math.

**Do NOT flag when** — cite the number when suppressing a finding:

1. decimals are read from the mint account (mint.decimals) and applied before any cross-mint comparison or division
2. a single mint is pinned by address in the program, the scale constant cites that mint, and no second mint can enter the math (an SPL mint's decimals are fixed at initializeMint and cannot change afterwards — but a CONFIGURABLE mint address with an assumed scale does NOT qualify)
3. the literal is not a token scale at all (compute-unit price, bps denominator, time constant) — name what it actually scales
